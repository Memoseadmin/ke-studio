# EP000 — Why Korean Ramyeon Is So Spicy (EN, ~250 words, dummy length)
Sources: research.md facts 1–3. Product (dummy): ramyeon multipack + yellow aluminum pot.

**S01 — Hook (0:00–0:15)**
In 2024, Denmark pulled three Korean instant noodles off its shelves. The reason? They were too spicy. So how did Korea end up making noodles that spicy in the first place?

**S02 — The mild era**
Go back to the mid-1980s. Korean instant noodle broths were mostly mild. Then, in October 1986, Nongshim launched Shin Ramyun, built around the flavor of a spicy beef and cabbage soup. Within a couple of years it reshaped the market, and spicy started to feel like the default.

**S03 — Turning up the heat**
In 2012, Samyang went further. Buldak, or "fire chicken," came out of an idea one employee had while eating a spicy chicken stir-fry at lunch. The team reportedly went through two tons of sauce and 1,200 chickens to get it right. It became the noodle behind countless online challenges.

**S04 — At the table (product moment)**
Here's the thing: in Korea, spicy ramyeon isn't a dare. It's a late-night meal, boiled in a thin yellow pot that heats fast, eaten straight from the lid. If you want to try it the way it's usually made, I've put the multipack and the pot I use in the description.

**S05 — CTA**
So next time a bowl makes your eyes water, you'll know it's forty years of Korean taste in one packet. Links are in the description, and tell me in the comments: Shin or Buldak?
